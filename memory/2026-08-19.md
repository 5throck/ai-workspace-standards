## Session Summary
docs(design): co-newbiz — add certification/regulatory response; promote deal analysis to a third team axis

## Changes
- `docs/designs/2026-08-19-co-newbiz-design_ko.md` — modified (§2 mission rewritten to three axes; §5.2/5.3/5.6 roster 32→33; §8.8-1 certification new; §8.9-1 deal-execution model new; §9 skills +7; §10 MVP tier + Stage 0.2 + meeting agenda M7-2/M7-3; §11 ADR 0053–0059; §13 success criteria; §14.8-1/14.9-1 verification; §15 open issues O-12–O-15)
- `CHANGELOG.md`, `memory/2026-08-19.md` — entries

## Decisions
- Deal analysis promoted from "explicitly not this team's job" to axis C, co-equal with governance (A) and system (B). This reverses the founding premise of the design and supersedes the 2nd-meeting R2 resolution (methodology-only + on-demand advisor).
- Axis C is justified as more than added scope: running real deals through the team's own procedures exposes procedural defects immediately, so C is the quality-assurance loop for A and B. Feedback path is explicit — defects surface as `interpretation` or `waiver` records, whose accumulation drives amendment priority (§6.6).
- Human/agent boundary fixed and marked non-negotiable: agents produce analysis, evidence, and a *proposed* RAG grade; gate approval and the investment decision stay with human approvers. `analysis_task.reviewed_by` NULL blocks the gate.
- Agents inherit the calling user's permissions and get no accounts of their own. A dedicated agent service account would be the single easiest way to defeat the whole §8.10 need-to-know model, so its absence is a verification item, not just a rule.
- Agent output must be stamped with agent/model/version/requester/reviewer. A number reaching IC whose origin cannot be traced is itself a governance failure.
- 2nd-meeting R10 (blanket tier downgrade after go-live) revised: tier now splits by work type, not phase. Routine monitoring drops to Medium; deal judgment keeps its stated tier, since misjudging valuation or certification transferability costs the same post-launch as during design.
- Certification kept strictly separate from permits — different issuers, procedures, and critical paths. Conflating them loses both. `certification.blocks_revenue` forces revenue to zero until mandatory certs are obtained, structurally preventing optimistic ramp-up curves.
- Certification transferability on change of control added as a mandatory M&A cross-check (5 → 6 validators). "Target already holds the certification" is a common valuation-premium argument, but several schemes require re-assessment on legal-entity change — if non-transferable, the premium disappears entirely.

## Open Issues
- O-12 (new): certification body and test-lab selection/cost — lead times and fees vary widely by body; needs survey once the target industry is fixed.
- O-13 (new): CoC transferability rules must be confirmed per certification scheme against the scheme's own regulations; cannot be assumed.
- O-14 (new): axis C creates standing workload that must be folded into the still-missing schedule/resource estimate (O-7).
- O-15 (new): no cap defined on concurrent deals, which is bounded by information isolation (wall_group), review quality, and agent cost.
- O-7 (carried): schedule and resource estimate still absent — now blocking O-9 and O-14.
