# Country Profile: US — United States of America

> **Advisory knowledge.** Verify against current statutes, tariff schedules (HTSUS),
> and FTA texts before relying on any statement here. This profile never auto-executes —
> agents load it at Phase 0 intake and treat it as jurisdiction context, not law.

## Overview

The US is the reference deep-import market: a single federal customs authority (CBP) with
published rulings databases (CROSS, eCBP), a mature but litigated classification culture, and
the world's densest FTA network by value (USMCA, KORUS, and 14+ partners). Trade-consulting
engagements anchor on HTSUS classification defensibility, USMCA origin qualification, and
post-entry customs hygiene (prior disclosure, reasonable care).

## Regulatory & Legal Framework

- **Customs authority**: U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP); classification under the
  Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS).
- **Export side**: EAR (BIS) for dual-use items; ITAR (DDTC) for defense articles — screening
  is a hard gate before any technical data leaves the country.
- **Partner-agency overlays**: FDA (food/drug/devices), FCC, CPSC — import readiness depends
  on the agency regime, not only customs.
- Route jurisdiction-specific legal opinions to the PM for domain-expert dispatch; this
  profile carries operational context only.

## Engagement Anchors

- HTSUS classification with CROSS ruling cross-references (classification defensibility is
  the primary audit surface).
- USMCA origin: product-specific rules by HS code; low-value de minimis threshold for
  section-321 entries changes cost models for e-commerce flows.
- Reasonable-care standard: importer of record carries the documentation burden — the
  deliverable must state which party holds IOR responsibilities.
